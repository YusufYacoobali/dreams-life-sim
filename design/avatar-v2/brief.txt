# Codex Master Plan — Avatar Artwork V2

**Repository:** `YusufYacoobali/dreams-life-sim`
**Branch:** `work`
**Priority:** High — visual quality and character identity

## Goal

Replace the current mediocre 2D avatars with polished, distinct cartoon characters matching `design/01-character-appearance.png`.

The latest implementation is an improvement over the old 3D portraits, but it still looks generic, childish and repetitive.

**The original reference is the visual target, not the current generated artwork.**

Do not redesign the UI, change gameplay or compromise character customisation.

## Phase 1 — Audit the current artwork system

Inspect:

- `src/assets/CharacterPortrait.tsx`
- `src/assets/portraitPlan.ts`
- `src/assets/portraitLayout.ts`
- `src/assets/portraitImages.ts`
- `tools/build-portrait-art.py`
- `assets/generated/avatars/`
- `src/ui/CreationElements.tsx`
- `app/appearance.tsx`

Identify which restrictions are causing repetitive faces and lost illustration quality.

Known problems:

- Four base face contours are not enough to create distinctive characters.
- Nearly identical eye shapes and expressions.
- Faces look too young and round.
- Only four clothing illustrations.
- Automatic masking and recolouring can flatten artwork detail.
- Outfit selection is tied to appearance traits.
- Some presets have different hair but effectively identical identities.

Document what can be reused and what should be replaced.

## Phase 2 — Create 16 genuinely distinct avatars

Generate a new coordinated set of 16 adult cartoon portraits inspired by the original reference.

### Visual direction

- Premium 2D mobile-game character artwork.
- Similar visual language to the supplied reference.
- Clean, intentional outlines.
- Soft, controlled cel shading.
- Attractive, friendly characters with natural proportions.
- Expressive faces without exaggerated childlike eyes.
- Properly illustrated hair, necks and shoulders.
- Consistent framing and lighting.
- Transparent backgrounds.

Avoid 3D renders, excessive gradients, glossy plastic skin, muddy colours, flat emoji faces and mismatched illustration styles.

### Individuality requirements

The 16 characters should have meaningful variation in:

- Face silhouettes and jaw shapes.
- Eyes, eyebrows and facial expressions.
- Nose and mouth shapes.
- Haircuts, lengths and textures.
- Skin tones.
- Clothing styles and colours.

Make characters feel like **16 different people**, not four faces with different wigs.

Keep the lineup diverse without locking particular appearances to gender identities.

### First quality gate

Before rebuilding the avatar system, produce:

1. A complete 4×4 grid of the 16 finished adult portraits.
2. A side-by-side comparison with `design/01-character-appearance.png`.
3. Four enlarged individual portraits to inspect facial detail.

Stop here for approval.

Do not proceed with mediocre results simply because image generation technically succeeded.

## Phase 3 — Build proper reusable character artwork

After artwork approval, construct a reusable layered illustration system.

Use individually authored, registered artwork layers rather than relying on approximate automatic segmentation.

Supported visual components should include:

- Distinct facial identities.
- Four face-shape choices.
- Skin tone variations.
- Front and rear hairstyles.
- Hair colours.
- Multiple illustrated outfits.
- Age-related facial changes.

**Important:** Do not reduce the approved character illustrations to generic vector shapes during integration.

Preserve antialiasing, line quality, shading and expressive details.

A hybrid approach is acceptable: independently illustrated facial bases combined with interchangeable hair, skin and clothing layers.

If a visual feature cannot be made modular without degrading quality, prefer a small set of curated artwork variants instead.

## Phase 4 — Preserve customisation and identity

Maintain all current options:

| Feature | Minimum |
|---|---|
| Avatar presets | 16 distinct |
| Skin tones | 6 |
| Hairstyles | 8 |
| Hair colours | 5 |
| Face shapes | 4 |
| Age stages | 8 existing stages |

Each control must cause the expected visible change.

Critically, changing hair colour must not change clothes or facial identity. Changing hairstyle should preserve the character's face.

If additional facial-identity data is needed, introduce it carefully, preserving backwards compatibility with existing saves. Existing characters must retain a stable, deterministic appearance.

Do not use gameplay randomness to select artwork.

## Phase 5 — Natural ageing

Develop age-specific artwork while retaining character identity.

- **Baby (0–2):** Clearly infant proportions.
- **Child (3–11):** Youthful facial features.
- **Teenager (12–18):** Transitional proportions.
- **Young adult (19–39):** Mature, attractive cartoon proportions.
- **Middle age (40–59):** Subtle age details.
- **Older adult (60–79):** Visible ageing and greying.
- **Elderly (80–94):** More pronounced ageing.
- **Very elderly (95+):** Advanced age details.

Ageing must be more than changing hair colour or stretching a head.

The person should remain recognisable throughout their life.

## Phase 6 — Integrate without UI changes

Replace the underlying avatar renderer while retaining its existing interface wherever possible.

Update portraits everywhere:

- Character creation.
- Main Life screen.
- Player profile.
- Relationships and NPC profiles.
- Family members and children.
- Saved-life and legacy screens.
- Any other portrait usage.

Keep the current screen layouts, controls, positioning and overall design unchanged.

Optimise artwork for offline Expo/React Native on Android and iOS. Avoid excessive simultaneous layers, memory usage or rerenders.

## Phase 7 — Visual acceptance and testing

Produce actual rendered screenshots, not just source artwork.

Required screenshots:

1. Full 16-avatar creation grid.
2. Four portraits enlarged to 140px.
3. Hair and skin customisation examples.
4. The same character at all eight age stages.
5. Main Life screen with the new portrait.
6. NPC and family profiles.

Compare these against the original designs and approved Phase 2 artwork.

Test:

- No artwork clipping or visible layer seams.
- Colours and shading remain accurate.
- All controls function.
- Saved appearances survive reloads.
- Existing save compatibility.
- iOS and Android rendering where environments permit.
- TypeScript and regression tests.

Do not report native visual testing as passed unless it was actually performed.

## Final acceptance criteria

The finished system must:

- Match the polish and art direction of the original reference.
- Deliver 16 visibly distinct characters.
- Look good at both small and large avatar sizes.
- Preserve the quality of the generated illustrations.
- Support full customisation and natural ageing.
- Work consistently for players and NPCs.
- Require no UI redesign or gameplay changes.

**Execution instruction:** Complete Phase 1 and Phase 2 first, then stop for my visual approval. Once approved, implement Phases 3–7. Do not invest in another full rendering system before we know the artwork is genuinely better.
